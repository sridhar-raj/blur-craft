blur-craft: with and without the skill

Each pair is the same brief given to the same model, once without the skill and once with it. The pages are live: hover, press play, click. Open them in Chrome to see the Liquid Glass refraction. github.com/sridhar-raj/blur-craft

Fairest test: one design, only the glass changes

The examples below let each agent design its own page. This one doesn't: one fixed harbour page, switch between glass written without a skill, by a generic public glassmorphism skill, and by blur-craft, with measured contrast for each.

1. Stillwater: meditation app hero

"A landing hero for a meditation app: calm and premium, a frosted glass player card over a slowly moving background."

Without the skill open ↗

  • 28px blur over a flat tint: the card reads as a grey slab
  • Plain 1px white border, no lit edge

With the skill open ↗

  • The sun is placed under the card, so the sunset (violet to peach) shows through the glass
  • Rim lit on the sun's side; the horizon bends through the lower edge (Liquid Glass)
  • 2px blur crossfade on play/pause and the breath cue

The skill wins on the glass: it's the most luminous object on the page instead of a grey box.

2. Tidal Room: music app with a now-playing dock

"The home screen of a music streaming app: a wall of album covers and a floating now-playing dock. Premium and immersive."

Without the skill open ↗

  • A dark, heavily dimmed dock: safe but flat
  • Elegant serif greeting

With the skill open ↗

  • The dock carries the covers' amber, magenta and teal through the glass, with an amber rim aimed at the light
  • The page tints itself from the playing cover; click any cover to change it
  • The ambient blur is baked once, so it holds 60fps (it was 22fps when blurred live)

The skill's dock is clearly richer glass. The version without it has the more refined greeting type: the skill only governs the glass.

3. Northbank: banking dashboard with an approval dialog

"A business banking dashboard: sticky top bar, balance, chart, transactions, and an 'Approve payment' dialog for £4,200 open over it. Premium, calm, trustworthy."

Without the skill open ↗

  • Light theme, a solid white dialog over a softly blurred page
  • Glass only on the sticky bar

With the skill (v2) open ↗

  • A dark glass dialog with a brass-lit rim over a focus veil
  • An extended-backdrop sticky bar with a glassy edge
  • Focus starts on the dialog, not on "Approve"

A draw, and the lesson the skill took from it: for money, a light, nearly solid dialog reads as more trustworthy than dark glass. The skill now matches the brief's register (immersive, calm or trusted) and has a glass--solid material for payments and deletes.

4. Atlas Stays: hotel booking over a photo

"A boutique hotel booking landing page: a full-bleed coastal town at golden hour, a sticky nav, a search bar and a featured stay card over the image. Premium and inviting, and every word must be easy to read."

Without the skill open ↗

  • Near-solid cream panels (90%): very readable, but they sit on the scene like stickers
  • Text on the photo protected by dark gradients

With the skill (v3) open ↗

  • Dusk-tinted glass that belongs to the scene; the card straddles the sun with a gold rim aimed at it
  • Liquid Glass on the search bar with a light prism fringe; extended-backdrop sticky nav
  • Every line measured against the brightest pixel behind it: 4.7:1 minimum, 9.5:1+ in the tinted level

The skill wins on atmosphere with readability measured, not guessed. This test also found that Chromium leaves Liquid Glass's rim unblurred when the blur is in the CSS chain; the asset now frosts inside the SVG filter.

5. Lumen: photo editor chrome over a photo

"The editing screen of a photo app: a large photo with a toolbar, tool rail, Adjust panel and filmstrip floating over it. Premium and professional, and every control must stay readable whatever the photo looks like."

Without the skill open ↗

  • Dark tinted glass with a brightness cut; vivid scene
  • Its author estimated ~6:1 contrast. Measured with the blur off (iOS Low Power Mode): 6 lines fail, lowest 3.35:1

With the skill (v4) open ↗

  • Thick, low-saturation glass so the chrome doesn't recolour the photo; toolbar grouped by function, Export the one solid primary
  • Measured on the worst frame pushed +2 EV: lowest 5.44:1 normal, 4.88:1 blur off, 6.7:1 high contrast
  • Rim angle sampled from the photo around each panel

Visually close; the difference is proof. The skill's build passes on the brightest photo in every state, the other fails in Low Power conditions. This test also produced neutral glass, a tinted level that only adds opacity, and a faster dialog veil.
